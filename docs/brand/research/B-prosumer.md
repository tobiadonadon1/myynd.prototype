# Research B: prosumer / builder AI breakouts (fetched 2026-09-30)

Method and limits. HTML/CSS pulled with curl (User-Agent set) for cursor.com, manus.im, krea.ai, suno.com, elevenlabs.io, raycast.com, bolt.new, tana.inc. Lovable, Gamma and Genspark sit behind a Cloudflare challenge (403): for those I read text through the r.jina.ai rendering proxy and read computed styles inside a real Chrome tab (Lovable and Gamma redirected to /it, so headlines quoted from those tabs are Italian; English headlines come from the proxy). Foundry names are only given when the fetched files or CSS show them; otherwise "foundry not verified". Revenue/launch facts come from WebSearch results, cited by URL, and are secondary sources. Anything I could not see is marked "not verified".

## Patterns (10)

1. **The prompt box or a download button is the hero, not a slogan.** Lovable, Bolt, Manus put a live input above the fold; Cursor, Raycast, Tana put a "Download" button and a product mock. Short imperative headlines: "Build something Lovable", "What will you build today?", "What can I do for you?", "Your shortcut to everything.".
2. **One brand across individual and enterprise is the default.** Cursor, Raycast, Bolt, Lovable, ElevenLabs, Krea, Manus all keep the same font, palette and nav; "Enterprise" is a nav link and a dedicated page, and the hero often carries two CTAs at once (Cursor: "Get started →" + "Request a demo →"; ElevenLabs: "Sign up" + "Contact sales").
3. **Enterprise voice changes nouns, not tone.** Lovable's "Every team ships", Cursor's "Develop enduring software at scale", Raycast's "without slowing your team down", Bolt's "Prototype to Production". The words shift to SSO, audit, control, but the sentence shape stays short. Manus is the exception ("Works like your best employee", "without adding headcount").
4. **Pricing ladder is nearly identical: free → ~$8-25 individual → ~$12-50/seat team → custom.** Raycast $8 / $12; Cursor $20 / $40; Bolt $25 / $30; Lovable $25 / $50; Gamma $9-90 seat; Manus $20 team. Team plans are cheap per seat and marketed as "everything in Individual, plus" (Cursor, Bolt, Lovable). Individual usage is the acquisition channel.
5. **Type is the cheapest way to look distinct, and the leaders use custom or paid faces.** Cursor (CursorGothic + Berkeley Mono), Lovable (Camera Plain Variable), Krea (Suisse Intl), Suno (Neue Montreal + Editorial New serif), ElevenLabs (Waldenburg weight 300), Gamma (ESBuild + PPMori). The lazy tier is Inter/Arial: Bolt (with one Silkscreen pixel accent), Raycast body, Genspark (Arial).
6. **Serif is the new "human" signal in AI.** Manus (Libre Baskerville italic on "Less structure, more intelligence."), Tana (Source Serif 4), Suno (Editorial New declared), Cursor (EB Garamond declared) all reach for a serif to offset the machine feeling. It reads distinctive against the sea of grotesk sans.
7. **Warm neutrals with one accent beat the purple-blue gradient.** Cursor (`#f7f7f4` / `#26251e` with `#f54e00`), Lovable (OKLCH hue 107 neutrals), ElevenLabs (`#FDFCFC`/`#EBE8E4`), Tana (`#fcfcfc`, orange `#f76b15`). Cold-black + purple glow is the default AI look; only Suno's black and Raycast's graphite-with-red earn it.
8. **What reads as generic AI slop**: "all-in-one AI workspace", "Effortless AI design", "Endless possibilities", "Bringing technology to life", a checklist of model names as a feature, numbered SEO tool lists (Genspark's DOM), and stock-looking gradient hero art. Two tells: model-name walls ("GPT Image 2, Nano Banana, Seedance...") and superlatives ("most powerful", "mind blowing") without a number attached.
9. **What is distinctive**: (a) product-as-the-page (Cursor's fake desktop windows over a painted wallpaper, Krea's home being the app, Suno's chart of real songs with play counts); (b) a single object metaphor (Raycast's keyboard with words on the keys, Raycast's honest "macOS Tahoe and Apple Silicon required" footnote); (c) a pun in the name (Lovable); (d) first-person product voice (Manus "What can I do for you?"); (e) proof as hard numbers ("48 hours", "€130K ARR in 30 days", "$2M+ savings", "99.8% crash-free rate", "83.4% Resolution Rate"). Cursor's quote wall of named power users is proof that money can't buy.
10. **How the best keep ONE brand from individual to enterprise**: keep one product demo and one visual system; let the top-nav "Enterprise" link open a page that re-uses the same components, swaps only the nouns and the CTA ("Get a demo" vs "Get started"); price the team plan as "everything in individual, plus"; publish security and control features as a list under the same voice (Raycast's AI Control Center, Lovable's "IT sees every app"); and keep the free personal tier legally usable at work (Raycast FAQ) so the bottom-up motion feeds the sales motion. Tana's meeting-memory framing ("Every call your team has adds to the same memory") is the closest existing copy for a digital-twin-to-company-brain story; nobody else in this set sells "memory" as the bridge.
