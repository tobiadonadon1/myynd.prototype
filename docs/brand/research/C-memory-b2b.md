## Patterns (10)

1. **The neighbourhood is being eaten.** Limitless went to Meta (5 Dec 2025, Rewind killed), Sana to Workday ($1.1B, Nov 2025), Poke to Cognition. Independent personal-memory brands vanish into platforms; a durable, owner-run, Mac-native brand is a real gap.
2. **Trust is signalled three ways, only one of them boring.** Boring: badge rows (Glean's ISO/HIPAA/SOC 2 strip, Sierra's five logos, Harvey's "SOC2 II Details"). Better: three-word promises (Lindy's "Compliant. Private. In your control.") and Day's scene ("when something looks wrong at 2 a.m., you're not the engineer on call... nothing went out without your approval"). Best: honest confession (Mem: "not end-to-end encrypted because Mem needs to process your knowledge"; Limitless's audio-retention dial).
3. **"Approval before action" is the universal trust hinge** (Lindy, Day, Attio, Sierra guardrails). None of them shows the approval as a designed, warm moment. That gesture is ownable.
4. **B2B AI slop is easy to spot:** "Complete context that makes AI work at enterprise scale", "trusted, personalised experiences across the entire customer lifecycle", "Superintelligence for work", "Better outcomes.", "agentic revenue". Abstract nouns, "platform", "context", "governance", metrics with no person, an indigo/blue accent (`#343ced`, `#5754ff`, `#345eb2`), Inter-adjacent grotesks, and "Get a demo" as the only door.
5. **Serif and warm grounds are the tell of the brands with taste:** Harvey (custom serif on ivory `#fafaf9`), Mem (cream `#fcf8f1` + navy + coral), Lindy (cream `#fcfbf8`), Poke (`#fffdfa` + Exposure), Day (P22 Mackinac + Berkeley Mono), Attio (Tiempos italic), Glean (Copernicus). Serif is now the default costume of "premium AI"; owning warmth needs more than a serif on cream.
6. **The only real "while you sleep" claim in the set is Attio's** ("Picks up leads at 2am. Catches renewals before they slip."), and Day's 2 a.m. audit trail. Nobody in the personal tier says "you wake up and it is done"; Mem says "chief of staff", Lindy says "teammate", Poke says "contact". Myynd can take morning as its scene.
7. **Personification is everywhere but characters are almost absent.** Day gives agents "names, job descriptions, and titles", Lindy calls it a teammate, Poke is a "contact", Hermes uses engraved Mercury and a "nous-girl" mascot file. Only Hermes and Poke carry real visual character. A mascot is genuine white space at the personal end.
8. **Poke is the closest tonal benchmark and it is a chat-first, phone-first, photo-warm brand** (beach photo, per-letter greeting, "keeps things as real as a friend"). Its weakness: no Mac depth, no proof of overnight work, now under Cognition.
9. **Funnel shapes split cleanly:** self-serve pricing with a "Free / $19-$30 / $60-$100 / $200" ladder (Lindy, Day, Mem, Poke, Hermes portal) versus demo-only (Glean, Harvey, Decagon, Sierra). Attio, Sana and Hermes are the hybrids (Start for free + Talk to sales; free account + Workday; install + Business/Enterprise). Same brand and page for individuals and teams works only at Lindy/Attio; nobody has a toggle. Tone hardly shifts, which means a "personal now, company brain later" brand can move up-market without changing voice if the voice is plain.
10. **White space for a warm, personal, frontier brand:** (a) a morning-after story with proof (a real "here is what I did while you slept" receipt, not a Slack mock); (b) local/private stated as a fact and a control, in one sentence, with a data-you-can-open motif (Lindy's "memory is plain files", Limitless's export were the only nods); (c) a visible, designed approval moment; (d) a character or typographic voice that is not lowercase-tech (Hermes and Poke are the only ones with edge; Day's ASCII is engineer-only); (e) no indigo, no chatbot bubble hero, no "platform".
