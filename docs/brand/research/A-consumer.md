# Research A: consumer / prosumer AI breakouts (fetched 2026-09-30)

Method: homepages and CSS fetched with curl; font files downloaded and their name tables read (fontTools) so foundries are real, not guessed; heroes rendered in headless Chrome (viewed by me); press via WebSearch/WebFetch. "not verified" = could not confirm from anything fetched today. Local raw files: scratchpad/h/ (HTML/CSS), scratchpad/shotsA/ (screenshots).

Headline finding before the detail: almost every site in this set has moved off its viral-era positioning. Cluely no longer says "cheat on everything"; Poke is now "joining Cognition"; Granola, Wispr, Dia and Littlebird all grew a work/teams/enterprise path. The consumer-to-company step you are planning is the normal trajectory, and the visible pattern is: same brand, same site, a second door.

## Patterns (10)

1. **Serif headline + grotesque UI is now the default premium-AI look.** Cluely and Wispr both use free EB Garamond over Geist/Figtree; Poke and Dia both license Exposure (205TF); Friend uses Miller Text; Littlebird pairs Meraki with Klim's Söhne; Sesame pairs Season Mix with ABC Diatype. Only Superwhisper and Cal AI stay pure sans. A serif alone no longer signals distinct; the pairing plus the specific face does.
2. **Warm, off-white grounds dominate; the accent is singular.** Cream `#ffffeb` (Wispr), `#fffdf5` (Littlebird), `#fffdfa` (Poke), `#fcfcf8` (Granola), `#FBFAF6` (Dia). Wispr lavender, Granola lime, Littlebird deep green. Cluely (blue sky) and Superwhisper/Friend/Sesame (dark photo) are the outliers, and they are the ones selling on feeling rather than on work.
3. **Photography or painted atmosphere replaces 3D and abstract AI blobs.** Friend (film), Sesame (lifestyle), Poke (coast), Littlebird (foliage), Dia (painterly blur), Cluely (illustrated sky). Nobody shows a glowing orb. The ones that show only product UI (Granola, Cal AI) read more "tool".
4. **The product UI is the proof, usually animated once.** Wispr's messy-to-clean transcript, Granola's typewriter chat, Cluely's overlay, Poke's iMessage thread, Dia's morning brief. No one has a working demo except Superwhisper ("press ⌥ + space and try it").
5. **Every work-adjacent brand grew a second door with the same brand, same domain, one new sentence.** Wispr `/business` ("The fastest teams don't type. They speak."), Granola `/enterprise` ("Turn meeting notes into company knowledge"), Littlebird `/for-teams` ("A personal AI assistant for every team member"), Dia "Dia for Work", Superwhisper Enterprise tier. Nobody made a second brand or subdomain; the consumer voice stays, the CTA becomes "Talk to sales".
6. **Pricing ladders converge on free / ~$15 / ~$35 per user.** Granola $0/$14/$35, Wispr $0/$15/$33, Littlebird $0/$17/$42/$100, Poke $0/$19/$199, Superwhisper $8.49, Dia $20. Free is always present and not hidden.
7. **The viral engines were not the website.** Cluely: a founder-starring launch video (13M+ views) and a manifesto; Friend: $1M+ subway ads that became graffiti; Sesame: a free voice demo; Cal AI: 250 paid influencers; Poke: X plus a text-a-number flow; Granola/Littlebird/Dia: operator-investor endorsements and Arc/newsletter audiences. Sites came after and got simpler.
8. **Provocation ages badly; personality ages well.** Cluely dropped "cheat on everything" for "#1 Undetectable AI for Meetings" and its founder said "maybe we launched too early". Friend's 2025 campaign drew hate, the 2026 relaunch uses quiet film photography. Poke's texting voice ("yeah i'm melting") is what Cognition paid for.
9. **Generic and sloppy tells**: leftover Tailwind default palettes (Cluely, Friend, Granola bundles), a trial font file shipping on friend.com ("ABC Favorit Unlicensed Trial"), a test Söhne cut on Littlebird, a stale "$81M" banner on Wispr after a $280M round, Cal AI's emoji and mid-fade hero, a PostHog experiment comment left in Littlebird's public HTML, and near-identical "trusted by / quote wall" strips everywhere. Taglines are mostly the literal category ("AI notepad", "always-on AI assistant", "AI for Meetings").
10. **What is distinctive**: Poke's footnote-numbered sections and one-glyph palm logo; Sesame's two-word "Curiosity, met" with no product shown; Wispr's ribbon of spoken text that literally becomes writing; Dia's giant swash "DIA" over a yellow ground; Granola's 5-line stacked serif headline plus dry humour ("Not the granola kind"); Littlebird's privacy section written as concrete negatives ("Banking skipped", "Gone means gone"). The recurring winning move is a named person or personality, and a claim that is a verb, not a noun.
