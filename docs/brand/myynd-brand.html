02

Strategy

The research left one scene unclaimed: the morning after. Everyone sells a helper you talk to. Nobody sells waking up to the work done. The name already holds the answer.

The brand idea

myynd is you, twice: a second you that learns how you work and does it through the night.

The two y's in the name are the two of you. One sleeps. One works. Every part of the brand is built from that pair: the wordmark splits into it, the site moves from night to morning, the mascot is the one awake.

Why Ledger lost

It is the most trustworthy and the least wanted. Receipts and monospace are how Day.ai already talks to engineers, and the research calls that register engineer-only. It serves the company brain, which does not exist until January 2027, and starves the twin, which ships now.

Why Plush lost

It is the most fun and the least safe. A product that reads your mail cannot lead with a cuddly face: Friend's persona read as a costume over “always listening”, and GitHub bans its mascot from money, security and enterprise pages. Plush has nowhere to go on the Companies path.

What Night Shift keeps from both

From Ledger, the receipt: every night ends in a list of what was done, in mono. From Plush, the twin: the creature survives as a small, silent indicator, not as the brand.

The system

Night Shift, built out. Every mark on this page is drawn from the same construction, so what you see here is the file, not a picture of it.

Logo · construction

One name, one letter twice.

The wordmark is drawn, not typed. Five letters on a single stem of 23 units over an x-height of 100. Both y's are the same glyph. The second one is the twin, in copper, 72 units to the right: close enough to tuck behind the first y, with a 6 unit gap cut where it passes behind you. That ligature is the one drawn detail no typeface has, and it says the idea without a word: a second you, right behind you.

Logo · clear space and size

One x of air on every side.

The clear space is the x-height of the wordmark at the size you use it. The wordmark never goes below 64 px wide on screen (x-height 13 px) or 18 mm in print; under that, use the symbol, which holds down to 16 px.

Typography

One family at five sizes, one mono for the clock.

The brand face is Forma DJR by David Jonathan Ross, the revival of Forma, released in 1968 by the Nebiolo foundry of Turin and drawn by Aldo Novarese and his team, who added small details to soften the rigidity of Helvetica. It comes in five optical sizes (Micro, Text, Deck, Display, Banner), so one family covers the 11 px caption and the 120 px headline, and the Italian origin is true rather than claimed. Times, sources and anything the twin writes down use Forma DJR Mono, because a timestamp should look like a record. This page renders them with the closest free proxies on Google Fonts: Schibsted Grotesk and Fragment Mono. Sources: Type Network, DJR, DJR Mono, Fonts In Use.

Why not a serif: 14 of the 32 companies in the research set their headlines in a serif, seven of them on a warm off-white ground (slop marker 1). The ivory, serif and terracotta myynd used until now is that look exactly. The serif was the thing that made the rest generic.

05

The twin, our mascot

Yes to a mascot, on one condition: it is an indicator with a face, not a friend. It shows what myynd is doing, truthfully, and nothing else. It never speaks.

Why yes

The biggest fear about a product that reads your mail is not boredom, it is “what is it doing right now”. A small face that is awake only while myynd reads answers that better than a settings page. OpenAI shipped the same idea as Codex pets in May 2026: sprites that show what the agent is doing and flag when a task needs you (Engadget). Characters are also nearly absent in this category: only Hermes and Poke have one (research C, pattern 7).

Why only as an indicator

Friend put a friendly persona on an always-listening pendant and it read as a costume over surveillance. Clippy failed by interrupting, and its large pupils read as leering (Seattle Met). GitHub bans its mascots from money, security, enterprise, apologies and crises (GitHub Brand Toolkit). So the twin reacts to app events, never to what a message says, and never looks at you.

Concepts explored

Four candidates. The one you already chose, with rules.

Live states

Every state is a fact about the app.

Pick a state. Each one is wired to something true: the eyes are open only while a source is being read, like a camera light. Idle blinks at random intervals so it never loops visibly, the trick Duolingo uses with Lily (Rive).

idle · nothing to read

Construction

Seven parts. The plush is the master, the flat rig is for small.

Body, belly, two antennae, two eyes, one grin, on a body 1 wide by 1.15 tall. The antennae carry the emotion, because at 16 to 40 px the eyes are two dots (the same reason Duolingo's wings are half circles, Apple Developer). The plush render stays the master for chat, the site and the film. Below 24 px and in the menu bar, where macOS wants a one-colour template image, the flat rig takes over with the same proportions.

Master: the plush render, rigged in three layers.

Sheet

Six states, side by side.

The menu bar

Where it lives: 18 px, one colour, always true.

Template image, so macOS tints it for light and dark bars. Frames: reading, working, needs you, paused. No bounce, no badge count, no sound.

Where it appears, and where it never does

Its places

  • The menu bar, as the state light.
  • Beside myynd's replies in chat, at 40 px.
  • Empty states and “all quiet” (not errors).
  • First run, once, when it wakes up.
  • The morning handover: one hop when the night's work is ready.
  • Marketing for individuals, the launch film, merchandise.

Never

  • Errors about data: sync, lost mail, wrong summaries.
  • Permission and consent screens.
  • Sign-in, keys, export, account deletion.
  • Prices, plans, limits, receipts.
  • The Send button or any irreversible action.
  • The Companies admin, audit and retention screens. Off by default for companies.
  • Apologies, incidents, outages, legal text.
  • Inside a draft, a summary or anyone's mail.

It has no name from us and no backstory. At first run you name it, as Finch lets you name its bird; until then it is “your twin”. Nobody writes lore for it (research D, rule 10).

06

Motion library

Only the twin moves. The rest of the mark stays still, so any movement at all means myynd is doing something. Every state below is the real wordmark, playing live.

One direction

The twin always travels to the right, the way time runs on the page. It never reverses in the middle of a gesture. Tobia asked for this on the working row on 21 September; the mark follows it.

One easing

Reveals use cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1), the app’s easing. Only the twin gets a small overshoot, because it is the one alive thing.

Motion means work

A loop on screen means a job is running. Nothing moves for decoration. When the work ends, the motion ends.

Reduced motion

Every state becomes its final frame, and the reveal becomes a 200 ms fade. Nothing is lost but the movement.

    Critique and accessibility

    The first build was reviewed as a hostile creative director would: what is weakest, what looks generated, what breaks its own rules. Ten findings, each fixed in the page you are reading. Then an automated WCAG audit in both themes and at phone width, run until it came back empty.

    The ten weakest points

    1. 01
      “Futura with one orange letter.”

      The first wordmark set the twin a full letter apart. Geometric sans plus one coloured letter is a 2019 direct-to-consumer cliché, and nothing in the drawing was ownable.

      Fixed

      The twin now tucks 14 units behind the first y, with a 6 unit gap cut where it passes: a yy ligature no typeface has, which says “a second you, right behind you”. Construction, icon and motion were redrawn from it.

    2. 02
      A peach orb in day mode.

      The cover’s glow survived into the light theme as a pale gradient blob: slop marker 10, and a break of our own rule that copper is light only at night.

      Fixed

      Every glow is tied to one token that is on at night and off by day. By day, copper is ink and the button, nothing else.

    3. 03
      The book broke its own motion rule.

      The mascot sheet’s “done” twin hopped every 3.2 seconds, forever. The rule on the same page says once per finished job.

      Fixed

      It hops once when it comes into view, and again only when clicked.

    4. 04
      The night demo hid its own story.

      Steps that had not happened yet sat at 22% opacity: 1.4:1 contrast. The most important prototype was unreadable until you dragged.

      Fixed

      Future steps are in the caption colour (5.7:1), finished steps get a copper time. The whole night reads before you touch it.

    5. 05
      Punctuation with holes in it.

      Tabular figures in the grotesk spaced commas and colons: “23 : 00”, “x-height , the unit”. It looked like a rendering bug.

      Fixed

      Tabular figures apply to numerals only. Clocks use the mono face.

    6. 06
      A card around everything.

      Stakes, markers, white space and mascots were all bordered boxes. When every block is a card, none of them is important.

      Fixed

      Table stakes became a ruled list. Cards are kept for things you compare side by side.

    7. 07
      “Needs you” looked like idle.

      At 120 px the difference was a 9° antenna tilt. A state light you cannot read is not a state light.

      Fixed

      Antennae up 15° and raised, body tilted 7°. It reads at the menu bar’s scale in the flat rig too.

    8. 08
      The hover faded to nothing by day.

      The twin warmed to glow on hover, which is 2.0:1 on the day ground. The mark lost its twin at the moment you pointed at it.

      Fixed

      By day the twin leans toward deep copper; at night toward glow. Same gesture, visible in both worlds.

    9. 09
      Direction heroes stretched into empty ground.

      Each direction’s hero grew to the height of its notes, 1,100 px of blank night with a headline in the middle.

      Fixed

      The hero keeps its size and stays in view while you read the notes beside it.

    10. 10
      Sloppy cascade.

      Two class names collided: the score total went ivory on ivory in light mode, and the motion captions turned into empty boxes. Exactly the “sloppy craft in public” marker we charge others with.

      Fixed

      Renamed, and the audit below re-run in both themes until clean.
